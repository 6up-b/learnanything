# Two principles

**Unconditional truths first.** Build on facts the learner can accept at face value: real definitions and
universal statements. Prefer the book's own definition, quoted and cited. Stress-test every root: is it really
acceptable as stated, or a theorem in disguise? A guess at a form (an ansatz) is allowed if you say it is a
guess and plan the step that verifies it; that verification is the payoff.

**Motivated discovery.** Every step answers a problem the learner can feel: why this move, why this form.
Nothing appears from nowhere. Ask yourself, for each step, how someone could have discovered it; the best
explanations make the learner feel they could have.
