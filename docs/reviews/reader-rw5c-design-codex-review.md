| F1 | High | Algebraic simplification can certify different function domains | **fixed** | `8bc4824d5` | **Reproduced** with an offline probe (`env -i`, empty `HOME`/`CODEX_HOME`/`LEARNLOOP_CONFIG_DIR`, `.venv` Python `-I`, sympy 1.14.0). `Fraction("0.0078125") == Fraction(float("0.0078125"))` is True. `simplify((x² − 1/16384)/(x − 1/128) − (x + 1/128))` gives `0`. `1/128` is not one of the 121 samples of `[0, 1]`. At `1/128` the source gives `nan` and the figure gives `1/64`. `solveset` gives the excluded sets `{1/128}` and `∅`. **Fix:** a new §6.3 step 3 collects each side's definedness conditions from the **unsimplified** ASTs. These are `/` (`≠ 0`), `sqrt` (`≥ 0`), `log` (`> 0`), `tan` (`cos ≠ 0`) and `^` (by exponent literal). Conditions that sympy proves true under the sign assumptions are dropped. For `equal`, the remaining conditions must be identical, or their excluded sets must be proved equal. A point in the symmetric difference proves difference, and a witness feeds the `differs` retry. Anything else is `undecided` (`domain`); parametric domains are deferred to P2. The breakpoint rule requires "both defined and equal, or both undefined". §6.4 requires no value shown outside the common domain. §3 item 2, G23c and §16 are updated. §14 adds the cancellation case, its reverse, `sqrt(x)^2` against `x`, a parametric-domain case and a positive-σ case to the planted-wrong corpus |
