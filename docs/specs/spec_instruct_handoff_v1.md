## 3a. IH0: the LLM gets time to answer

**What the log shows.** The five objective plan decisions' LLM calls took 11.6-15.8 s and all completed, while a serving step waited 8 s. On Nov 7 the phone's three decisions gave up 1.5-7 s before the answer arrived. The two desktop decisions on Nov 8 were in time because the job had long finished. Five calls are a small sample.

1. **The wait is 40 s.** `router.timeout_seconds` defaults to 40, and the owner's three vaults set it (owner decision, Nov 8). It is operational in the parameter registry, with no measurement authority. `prefetch_timeout_seconds` stays 45. A serve stops waiting as soon as the job finishes, so 40 s is reached only by a stalled call.
2. **The phone starts the job while feedback shows.** The desktop's prefetch is `next_plan_item{serve: false}` (`app/dailyPlan.ts:105`). The phone makes no peek, on purpose: a read at a finished block crosses a boundary and writes its decision (`apps/learnloop-mobile/src/api/phone.ts:127`, `spec_mobile_frontend_v2.md` §4.5). On Nov 7 the phone sat on feedback for about 100 s, then started the call when Next was pressed.
   - Add a host RPC, `plan_prefetch{sessionId}`, that **starts** the boundary's objective job under the phone's device filter and origin, then returns at once.
   - It writes no `router_decisions` row, block event, presentation or serve, and it returns no step. It is idempotent, and it is a no-op when the next step is not a boundary or no model is set up.
   - The phone calls it after the grade lands and while feedback shows, never earlier: the decision depends on the outcome just graded (§4.5). A failed call is ignored.
   - Next's existing serve then finds the job by its prefetch key. The key must match the serve's, or the prefetch is wasted, so a test pins that.
   - If the key cannot be computed without a write, stop and report instead of relaxing "writes nothing".
   - **As built.** `daily_plan/engine.py::prefetch_boundary` walks `advance_with_context`'s path to the chooser on a read-only attach (`Repository.attach(read_only=True)`), so a write fails at the database, with the chooser `start_only` (`objective_chooser.py`). `reconcile`'s events are folded over the stored ones in memory. The reply's `reason` says why nothing started. What only a write can settle ends the walk and the serve decides as before: a moved reading, the day's first randomized-check draw or a check placed at this boundary, an AD deferral re-read, an offer the sweep would mark `ignored`, a waiting follow-up. `due_followup_tasks`' two lazy status marks are skipped on a read-only attach; they change nothing it returns. The host also no-ops while the session's diagnosis is out and the phone fires once it settles, since the serve waits for that diagnosis and what it finds can change the key.
   - **As built (the seed, on merging router speed stage 2).** Speed 2 derives the numeric policy's seed from the boundary's identity, the dict the prefetch key hashes (`objective_chooser.numeric_seed`, its landing ruling R2). The prefetch and its serve compute one identity, so one key and one seed, and the job runs once. Every decision path IH added carries the seed of its own identity, and at a fixed draw count that seed determines its values: IH0's start-only prefetch, IH2's boundary contest and its `plan_interrupt` lesson arm on both paths, with or without room to wait. IH3's options and override draw nothing, so they have no seed. Nothing in `src/` changed for this; `tests/test_ih_numeric_seed.py` pins it (`c6e2e8d3f7`).
3. **What remains.** Steps with nothing to start early, such as Keep going, wait up to 40 s on "Choosing what's next…". The cost of the prefetch is an LLM call for a boundary the learner may not reach, as on the desktop.

## 5. IH2: offers and follow-ups compete in one decision (algorithm change)

Follows `docs/algorithm-change-playbook.md`: new `mvp` tag and replay.

1. **Redraw instead of withdrawing.** When a follow-up becomes ready while an offer is open, the next boundary makes a new decision whose support holds both. The unanswered offer's response records `superseded_by_decision`, and the plan's act stays out of the acceptance model, as withdrawal does today.
2. **Forced follow-ups become candidates** at a boundary where a lesson is available. The objective and the two-policy mix choose, with exact logged propensities. Items the learner requested and randomized checks stay forced: those are the learner's act and the experiment's design.
3. **After a miss, the post-feedback Next is a real decision.** `plan_interrupt` gains the lesson offer as a candidate, and the router draws. This is the only way a lesson can follow feedback in mid-block, since plan decisions otherwise wait for block boundaries. The router already decides before the item is generated (`followup_run/router.py`), so a lesson that wins costs no generation.
4. **A value for probes.** The Nov 7-8 traces only hold the kinds `review`, `new_practice`, `repair`, `stop` and `lesson`. The build must confirm against RO §8.1 whether a probe interface exists and add one if not, using RO's existing machinery with no new constants.
5. **Latency.** The offer uses IH0's wait and the prefetch's existing budget. A call that still fails falls back as IH5 says. Router speed stage 2 is separate.

- **Switch.** `[router_objective] lessons_compete_with_followups` (default true; registry: structural/operational kill switch). Gate `router_objective_switches.lessons_compete_with_followups_on`: the tag, lesson offers on (so the objective serving) and the switch. Off, or below the tag, `lesson_offer.py`'s forced rule and the withdrawal run as mvp-0.54's. The prefetch key names the switch from the tag on. It is not in RO6's generator payload: it changes which decisions hold which arms, and a decision's own trace says so (`followup_contest`, `lesson_offers`). No migration: `router_decisions` has no CHECK on candidate kinds or refs, and the workflow's states and fields already exist.
- **Item 1, redraw.** A follow-up ready while an offer is open crosses the boundary with the offer still open (`engine.advance_with_context`, `_boundary(superseding=)`). The new decision's own transaction ends the old block `superseded` and writes an `expired` row with `superseded_by_decision`. A `DecisionPending` read leaves the offer open. The row is the plan's act, as a withdrawal: the acceptance model and the decline recovery read neither, the sweep never calls it `ignored`, and a late Start is refused.
- **Item 2, the contest.** With a lesson available the forced follow-ups become one candidate (`followup_contest:<first item>`, a repair block holding only them), and the support is exactly `[follow-up, offer]`. The owner's decision sets a lesson against a follow-up, so no other block competes and stop is not in this support (a drawn stop never displaced a forced item either, so the coin is not flipped here). Staged's pick is the follow-up, so with both policies failed the lesson keeps only the floor, and a chooser failure places the follow-up. Requested items, checks (`requested`, `check_kind`) and RO5's check block stay forced, the latter before any decision. A follow-up whose own decision already held a lesson and drew the follow-up stays forced: that draw stands and is not run twice (`engine._lesson_already_competed`).
- **Item 3, after a miss.** `plan_interrupt` gains the offer on both paths: the workflow's (`ObjectiveFollowupGate.decide_followup`, before anything is generated) and the need's mid-block one (`ObjectiveRestraintGate.decide`). With no room to wait the support is `{insert_now, lesson}`, a real draw; with no lesson it stays deterministic `staged_v1`, as before. No arm while an offer is open (the follow-up then meets it at the boundary, item 1), over a reading, in a check block or over a probe. A lesson that wins generates nothing: the author is never called. The drawn offer gets a block of its own in the decision's transaction, so feedback's Next opens IH1's page. With questions left in the block it `interrupts`: it comes first (never over an open item) and the block resumes after the answer. Its row is `decision_kind = plan_interrupt`; the store reads it with `plan_block`'s offers (`offer_block_id` names its block), so acceptance, ceiling and recovery count it.
- **Item 4, a value for probes.** RO §8.1's probe bundle exists (`router_objective/value.py`, `V.PROBE`) and was never reached: follow-ups were not candidates, and FG1's stand-in has no item, so it was valued as a repair. No interface was added. A generated probe in a contest gets the bundle through its item's mode; in `plan_interrupt` the stand-in is annotated (`followup_probe`) to get it. Only when a lesson is in the support: without one, the values are mvp-0.54's. One new field, `Action.declined`, points an offer's declined branch at the follow-up it competes with, which stays available, instead of the tail's next block. No new number: the same draws, sensors and acceptance Beta.
- **Item 5, latency.** The same 40 s wait and prefetch budget; a failed policy falls back as IH5 says.
- **Held feedback.** A lesson win releases what is held at once (`release_reason = lesson_offer_won`) and sets `exposed_at`, as for a wait, so a probe served later can't settle the cause. The workflow moves to `waiting` with `chosen_ref = lesson_offer:<LO>`, and its event names the offer's decision. It is not terminal, because the follow-up is still owed a decision (below). Next goes on: nobody waits on it.
- **After the answer** (`engine._settle_lost_followups`, read from the offer's ledger however it ended). Start or an alternative: the workflow settles, reason `lesson_taken`, and a probe already written retires. Not now, `ignored` or superseded: a probe already written goes on as any wait does, revalidated, then at the boundary it competes if a lesson is available and serves if not (the Nov 3 behaviour). With nothing written, the workflow goes back to `pending` and its one job runs again for a new decision, on a new receipt whose `parent_decision_id` names the one it lost. A need's item never left the pool; `followups_lost` on the offer's block keeps it from re-running the contest while the offer is open.
- **The phone.** No offers in a phone decision's support (`none:device_serves_no_lesson`), and no lesson arm on a miss whose workflow's surface is the phone: an arm it can only hold would take a share of its decision from what it can serve. An offer the desktop drew stays held for the desktop, and a follow-up on the phone serves beside it, neither answering nor replacing it. Mobile V6 revisits this.
- **Test against rules** (`tests/test_ih2_lessons_compete.py`). Static: no condition in the decision modules names a score, a miss count or a stuck flag, from a word list. Behavioural: a zero and a full answer give the same support, and either arm serves under either. Limits: it can't see a rule under another name, or a rule inside a model's prompt. `lesson_suggestion.build_candidates` does read scores (`cold_failing`), by design: it says what is offerable, and the offer then has to win the draw.

## 7. IH4: how preferences inform the non-LLM parts

Two facts stay separate. "I would take this" is about acceptance. "This would help me" is a claim that needs checking against outcomes.

1. **Acceptance:** an unprompted request for a lesson updates TE1b's acceptance model, flagged as unprompted. That model shows `updates: 0` today.
2. **Choice model:** `P(chosen kind | decision features)`, with seeded priors and the same form as the acceptance model, frozen as a sensor in the decision trace.
3. **Entering the value:** through one learned coefficient, `w_pref`, which starts at 0 like RO6's `w-hat` and is fitted on cold-outcome residuals. A preference shifts the numeric policy only as far as the outcome data supports. No hand-set bonus for following the learner.
4. **What overrides can't show:** picks are self-selected and carry no propensity, so they stay out of `w`'s scoring and out of any causal claim. Comparisons of an override with a router draw of the same action carry RO5's `self-selected` label.
5. **Report:** a `learnloop data-capture report` section with router-versus-learner agreement by kind, override rate, follow-through and later cold results.
6. **The LLM's context (owner decision, §8.3):** the router's LLM card carries recent preferences and notes as context, marked as the learner's own statements.

**As built (IH4, `instruct/handoff`; the same unreleased tag, `mvp-0.55`).** `router_objective/preferences.py` (the choice model, `V_pref`, `w_pref`), `router/lesson_suggestions.py` (the unprompted offset), `daily_plan/objective_card.py` (the card), `router_objective/preference_report.py` (the report). No migration: everything is computed from the ledgers, and the inputs a replay needs are frozen in the preference's own `offered_json`.

- **Switch.** `[router_objective] preferences_inform_router` (default true; registry: structural/operational kill switch). Gate `router_objective_switches.preferences_inform_router_on`: the tag, the objective serving and the switch. Off, or below the tag, the acceptance model, the numeric policy, the trace, the card and the report are as IH3 left them, and `offered_json` is IH3's bytes.
- **Item 1, acceptance: an offset, no weight.** A request enters TE1b's model as one more observation with its own effect in `h`, `unprompted`, prior N(0, 1²): the same width as every effect (`PRIOR_SD`, basis legacy), centre 0, so no prior claim that a request differs from a Start. An offer's `h` holds 0 there. So how far a request moves `P(accept)` is the posterior's split between the offset and the effects an offer reads, and the more offers have been answered, the more of the step goes to the offset (a test pins it). The model's form can take this with no hand-set weight. The slot exists only once a request is on record, so with none every array and number is TE1b's, bit for bit; with one the priors are named `suggestion_priors_v1+unprompted_v1`, and the trace's `p_accept` and the model's summary say `unprompted_updates`. What counts: a preference of kind `lesson` with frozen inputs, including a phone's (`waits_on_desktop`). What doesn't: a choice of the offer the plan had drawn (an agreement: the offer was on screen, and its Start or "Not now" is the response, so this would count it twice), a row written with the switch off, a lesson the learner started elsewhere (the ledger is the only source). Read only in `lesson_offer.build`. TE1b's daily draw reads nothing: where it runs the objective isn't serving the plan.
  **Limits.** A request is only ever `accepted`: moments the learner could have asked and didn't are not refusals, so the data is one-sided and the offset is what absorbs that, not a correction for it. With requests and no answered offers, the intercept keeps rising (sublinearly, from a prior predictive already at 0.9). A request does not clear a recent "Not now" on its topic.
- **Item 1 against item 3: a tension in the spec, decided.** `P(accept)` values an offer's branches, so a lesson request moves that one offer's `Q̄` even with `w_pref` at 0. "`w_pref` at 0 changes nothing" therefore holds exactly for every other preference and for the value's `w_pref` channel; the one number a lesson request moves at 0 is the offer's acceptance. The two facts stay separate because they go through separate channels (a test pins both).
- **Item 2, the choice model.** `P(kind | the link was used here, features)`: per kind a Gaussian over five effects (`intercept`, `hour_sin`, `hour_cos`, `minutes_short`, `last_miss`), AD's assumed-density step, the Gaussian-sigmoid mean, normalized over the decision's kinds. `last_miss` is 1 − the latest graded answer's correctness (through the availability predicate, no age limit): a learned effect, no rule. Seeded `choice_priors_v1`: TE1b's width on every effect, each centre 0 but the intercept's, which is where a kind's predictive is one in six (`LS._centre_for`, structural). One function reads the features at a decision and froze them on the preference (`offered_json.choice_features`). Frozen in the `plan_block` trace as `preference` (`choice`: `p`, `updates`, `rows`, `features`, `self_selected`; `candidates`: kind, `p`, `v_pref`). With nothing on record the trace gains no key. `plan_interrupt` has no sensor and no `w_pref` (as it has no residual: another action space).
- **What each row contributes.** A pick agreeing with the plan's draw wrote no `learner_pick`, and it still counts as one choice among what was offered: it says as much as an override. A preference with no draw counts too: the model is over kinds given features and needs no draw. Only the report tells them apart.
- **Not using the link isn't a choice.** No row exists for a decision where it wasn't used, and none is invented, so the model is conditional on use and the served action is never an implicit choice. **Limits:** it can't say how much the learner wants what it favours, or that they were content with what was served; and use is itself selected (more likely when something feels wrong), so it under-represents what they'd have accepted silently.
- **Item 3, the value.** `Ṽ = V_tail + ŵ·V_residual + w_pref·V_pref` on the numeric argmax, at `plan_block`. `V_pref` is the frozen `p` of each candidate's kind on the tail's own scale: standardized over the candidates, times the spread of their `Q̄`. So `w_pref` is dimensionless like `ŵ`: at 1, one spread of the learner's model is worth one spread of the tail's, and that is as far as it can go. No new number: RO6's `residual_weight_prior_sd` and clip to `[0, 1]` mean here what they mean there (in raw probability they would have said a preference could be worth a whole use, and a simulated null moved the choice). `w_pref` is `learned_tail.residual_weight`: slope, rank, least favourable fill. With `w_pref` at 0 nothing is shifted and the tie record is unchanged; `Q̄` in the trace stays the tail's whatever it is. Without RO6's learned tail on, the sensor freezes and `w_pref` isn't fitted.
- **What it reads.** Every `plan_block` draw with a frozen sensor with something on record, some spread and a closed window. With `π` the exact served distribution (under the coin too) and `d(a) = V_pref(a) − Σπ·V_pref`: `Ĉ = d(A)·Ĝ`, against `Σπ·d·Q_tail` (flat coefficient, as RO6's) and `Σπ·d²`. Then `E[Ĉ | x] = α·Σπ·d·Q_tail + b·Σπ·d²`: a state's level cancels and `b` is the slope. `Ĝ` is RO6's window return through the randomized checks, each term `Y − m⁰`, with its missing terms bounded. Unlike RO6's coin this uses the mix's draws, so it reaches every kind; RO6's §10.1 left them out only because its spec named the coin. One addition: `residual_weight(noise_dof=True)`. RO6's cohort holds the `policy` arm at zero; here every row is a real draw, so with few rows a fit through them reported no noise and dropped the prior (one draw could reach half of `w_pref`'s range). Off (RO6's path), its bytes are unchanged.
- **Item 4, what `w_pref` can't show: pinned.** It is fitted only on rows that aren't self-selected. A `learner_pick` has no trace and no propensity, so none is in its cohort; a drawn decision the learner overrode stays as what was drawn (intention to treat: dropping it would select on what the draw led to). `w`'s scores read the traces alone. **It can say:** where the mix happened to draw a kind the learner's choices favour, cold results beat the tail's forecast by this share of the tail's spread. **It can't say:** whether a pick works; whether following the learner works because they asked (the draw serves regardless of what they would have said then); whether kinds differ (one coefficient); anything about what the mix gave only the floor; anything on the cutoff's scale (the window's return, as RO6's). And if the model's reading tracks the tail at a constant correlation, it can't separate the two (the flat coefficient takes it).
- **What its data looks like, plainly.** It stays at 0, and is likely to for a long time. Today: no preference exists (IH3 is unreleased), so no sensor, no contrast, `w_pref` exactly 0. What would move it: a preference, then decisions freezing a sensor, their 14-day windows closing, and a positive slope at the least favourable fill. Simulated on synthetic draws, not this learner's data: with a bounded missing term in every window (range ±0.5 of the return) it stays ≈0 whether the true effect is 0 or 0.5, even at 300 draws, and a window with no missing term needs a session every day and every drawn check completed. With no missing terms and noise 20 times the tail's spread it can't tell the two apart at 300 draws (mean 0.24 against 0.30), so a null can move it (90th percentile 0.85). One learner's windows will almost always hold a missing term, which is what keeps that from happening, and the bound's share of the data is not measured on a real log.
- **Item 5, the report.** `learnloop data-capture report`, `learner_preferences` (at the gate): agreement by kind (router drew against learner chose, agreements counted), override rate (of preferences with a draw and of plan decisions), follow-through (a question answered, a lesson opened on the topic, a reading read, a stop), later cold results by kind (override, agreed, no draw, the router's draw), where `w_pref` stands and what would move it. Every part is labelled with RO5's `self-selected`. An override is counted from the preference (served ≠ parent), and the rest of the plan's decisions are never read as agreement.
- **Item 6, the card.** `router-policy-card-v3`, only on a card that holds statements; with none it is v2, byte for byte. `history.learner_statements` is one more section, under the limit of the learner's questions (`ROW_LIMITS["questions"]`, no window of its own) and then the shared `llm_card_token_budget`, oldest first, `left_out` saying what went. Each entry: `source: "the learner's own statement"`, `said_would_help`, `where`, `plan_had_chosen`, `topic`, `days_before`, an evidence id `preference:<id>` the rationale may cite, and `note`. `learner_statements_are` says at the top that this is context, not evidence, unchecked against outcomes, and that a decision with no entry says nothing. A note follows the card's rule for the learner's text: on an attempt `learner_protection.PastWork` protects (or an attempt it can't find) it shows `note_withheld` with the reason and no text, and with protection unreadable every note is withheld. No note on an attempt shows, as a question with no attempt does. The chosen kind and topic always show. The mid-block `plan_interrupt` LLM request gets the same entries as text lines; the Decider's state keeps its bytes. The trace's `learner_statements` names the card, the entries and what the budget left out.
- **The key and the seed.** `objective_settings` names the switch only while it serves (off, the key keeps its bytes). Every objective identity names the ledger's mark (`preferences: {n, latest}`) when any row exists, so a new preference starts a new job. `numeric_seed` takes both out (`SEED_NEUTRAL_OBJECTIVE_KEYS`, `SEED_NEUTRAL_IDENTITY_KEYS`), since neither moves a value at a fixed draw count. Speed 2's own tuple is for switches inside the follow-up snapshot, which this isn't (that would read true at `mvp-0.50`-`0.54`). IH0's prefetch reads the mark on its read-only attach and computes the serve's key and seed (tested). Not in RO6's generator payload: as IH5 and IH2, it changes who is drawn, not `Q̄`. Exception to flag: with `sensors_in_value` on (default off) the LLM's forecasts enter `Q̄`, and they now read a richer card.
- **Staged reads nothing.** No staged module names the ledger, and with the objective off a whole run passes with its readers set to fail (tested).
- **Limits.** (1) Not verified against a real log or in a running app: no vault was read. (2) A note with no attempt behind it could quote anything the learner typed; that is the card's existing rule. (3) A lesson request only records that it was asked: whether the lesson opened is the report's follow-through, not the acceptance model's. (4) Reading the sensor costs a replay at each decision with any preference on record, and `w_pref` a parse of the cohort's traces; with none on record, one `SELECT`.
- **Where the tests are.** `tests/test_ih4_preferences_inform_router.py`.

## 7a. IH5: the LLM's share falls to numeric, not staged (algorithm change)

Follows `docs/algorithm-change-playbook.md`: its own `mvp` tag, a kill switch and replay. It amends the failure rule in `spec_router_objective_v1.md` §8.3, "Each failure moves only that policy's share" to staged.

**Why.** RO gives staged no serving share, yet it served 3 of the 5 objective plan decisions on Nov 7-8, each because the serve stopped waiting for an LLM that then answered. Numeric answered all five and disagreed with staged on those three. Staged can never pick a lesson (`daily_plan/objective_chooser.py:1145`), so whenever the LLM has no valid answer in time, 0.8 of the mix can't choose instruct. Five decisions from two days are a small sample: this shows what can happen, not a stable rate.

**IH0 comes first and should make this rare.** IH0 removes the cause seen on Nov 7. IH5 is the backstop for what remains: a stalled call, an invalid answer or a failed card build.

**The change** (`src/learnloop/router_objective/mix.py:39`):

| Failed | Today | After IH5 |
|---|---|---|
| Neither | LLM `w`, numeric `1 − w` | The same |
| LLM | Staged `w`, numeric `1 − w` | Numeric 1.0 |
| Numeric | LLM `w`, staged `1 − w` | LLM 1.0 |
| Both | Staged 1.0 | The same |

The uniform floor `ε` applies in every row, and propensities stay exact.

**What stays:** staged remains the last resort when both policies fail, and its pick is still logged on every decision as the baseline. Its ordering still limits the top-k support and breaks numeric's ties (§9).

**What this costs.** `π_Q` is an argmax, so with no LLM answer a decision is almost deterministic apart from the floor. That is less exploration than the LLM's distribution gives, and the same concentration staged has today. Numeric's picks are also largely untested on real outcomes.

**Replay's limit.** Replay can recompute each logged decision's `μ` under both rules (`mix.py:118`) and report how often the served choice would change. It can't show that numeric's picks would have taught better. The data capture report splits outcomes by `drawn_component` from this tag on, so the comparison builds up after landing.

**As built (IH5, `instruct/handoff`; the tag is one constant, `FAILURE_SHARE_ALGORITHM_VERSION = "mvp-0.55"`, renumbered at landing if another successor lands first).**

- `router_objective/mix.py`: `effective_weights(w, failed, *, to_other_policy=False)` gives the table's rows; `recompute` reads the rule from the logged `mixture.failure_rule` (`"other_policy"`; a record without the key, drawn below the tag or with the switch off, replays under staged's rule, so every older decision still replays to its logged `μ`).
- Switch `[router_objective] failure_share_to_other_policy` (default true, registry: structural/operational kill switch). Gate `router_objective_switches.failure_share_to_other_policy_on`: the tag, the objective serving and the switch. Off, or below the tag, the mix is mvp-0.54's and the record gains no key.
- Both objective call sites (the plan block's and the mid-block follow-up's mix, `daily_plan/objective_chooser.py`) pass the gate. The key is logged in `mixture` and in the trace's `mixing`. The prefetch boundary key (`objective_settings`) names the switch from the tag on, so a flip starts a new job; below the tag its bytes are unchanged. The switch is not in RO6's generator payload: it changes who is drawn, not any `Q` value the learned tail fits.
- Unchanged: staged's pick is logged on every decision (`staged_ref`, `model_distributions`), orders the top-k support and breaks ties; `fallback` and `fallback_reasons` name the policies that failed (as before), never who served. `drawn_component` now names the policy that actually drew (`numeric` or `llm` under a lone failure, where it said `staged`). The router objective report adds `policy_share_when_a_policy_failed`, the draw's component among decisions with a failed policy, because from this tag `numeric` in `policy_share` mixes numeric's own share with a lone LLM failure's. The glass-box sentence for a missing LLM answer says the estimate took its share when the rule is the new one.
- A decision where a policy failed still carries no score for `w` (RO4's rule, unchanged), so the fallback share does not move `w`.
- Replay: `tests/test_ih5_successor.py` recomputes `μ` under both rules and, on a synthetic history, draws every decision's seed under both and counts where the served choice differs (only decisions with exactly one policy failed can differ).

## 7b. IH6: an open workbench is an open item (algorithm change)

Under the unreleased `mvp-0.55` tag, with its own kill switch in `[router_objective]`. Off or below the tag, reads behave as they do today.

**Why.** On Nov 8 the plan engine didn't know a workbench was open, so a background plan read drew the decision at 12:37 pm, before the failed ReLU attempt at 1:08 pm existed.

1. **What counts as open:** a workbench session whose status is open, for the plan's current practice session. The plan engine learns this through one small read, in the shape it already uses for facts about the plan.
2. **Non-serving reads don't cross a boundary** while one is open: the desktop's global plan query, Today's poll and `next_plan_item{serve:false}` write no decision. They report what the plan already holds, and the plan view says plainly that a question is in progress instead of "Choosing what's next…".
3. **No job starts for that boundary either,** including the phone's `plan_prefetch`, since its key would go stale when the answer lands. `plan_prefetch` reports this as a new no-op reason. Closing the workbench lets the next read or prefetch start the job as usual.
4. **A serve still decides.** An explicit step (Next, Begin, Continue) always decides, so this can never strand the learner.
5. **A workbench left open must not stall the plan.** On Nov 7-8 one stayed open across a day. A session left open beyond the current practice session doesn't count. The build must also check that the learner can always leave or resume it from the plan view. No new time limit is added: if a limit seems necessary, stop and report.
6. **The cost:** every boundary decision, not only lessons, can come later and use fresher evidence. That changes what the router decides and when, so the trace must show the decision's own time and evidence as it does today.

**As built (IH6, `instruct/handoff`; the same unreleased tag, `mvp-0.55`).**

- **Switch.** `[router_objective] workbench_is_open_item` (default true; registry: structural/operational kill switch). Gate `router_objective_switches.workbench_is_open_item_on`: the tag, `workbench.enabled` (off, nothing can resume the problem) and the switch. It needs no objective, because any chooser's boundary crosses on a read. Off, or below the tag, `open_workbench` answers None before reading anything, so the reads and their replies are mvp-0.54's. No migration, and nothing replay reads changes.
- **Not in the prefetch key, so not in `SEED_NEUTRAL_SWITCHES`.** It changes when a boundary is decided, not what that boundary's job computes, so a job from before a flip is still right for its boundary. The tuple strips switches that are in the key's identity from the numeric seed; this one is not in the identity, so the seed doesn't move and there is nothing to strip. Not in RO6's generator payload either: `Q` is unchanged.
- **What counts as open, and a correction to item 1.** `workbench_sessions.practice_session_id` is filled at submit, so an open problem names no session. A problem belongs to the current session when it is `open` and `updated_at >= sessions.started_at`: opened, resumed or worked since the session started (`db/stores/daily_plans.py::open_workbench_since`). The current session is the one the read names while it is open, else the vault's open one; at most one is open (`start_session_exclusive`). With none open, nothing holds. The read lives in the plan's store, not `stores/workbench.py`, because `evidence-never-reads-the-workbench` forbids an indirect import from `scheduling`, `router` and the rest, and they import the plan engine. It reads no evidence: it decides when, not what.
- **One addition to item 1.** A problem whose item has an attempt since its last touch holds nothing. Steps left for the answer box keep the problem `open` and resumable, and an answer there doesn't close it, so without this the plan would say a question is in progress after it was answered. Working the problem again makes it count again.
- **`status` is the whole test of "open"; "Leave for now" doesn't lift it.** `workbench_left` is logged when the learner navigates away, including to look something up mid-question, so lifting on it would reopen the Nov 8 failure. A problem outside the plan counts, and so does a Reader problem whose import hasn't made it an item yet (the owner: "an open workbench").
- **Reads (`engine.advance_with_context`, `serve` false).** Held, a read decides nothing and starts no job: no first block of the day (`build_plan(first_block=False)`, the plan row stands with no block, the shape a pending decision already leaves), no `_insert_followups` (so no `plan_interrupt`, no `followup_refused`, nothing placed), no boundary. At a boundary it returns a `question_in_progress` step. The view has `deciding: false` and `question_in_progress`, a key present only then, so every other reply keeps its bytes. In mid-block it returns the step in hand, as before. `reconcile` still runs, as on any read: its events aren't decisions. `plan_keep_going`, `set_plan_minutes`, `plan_reading_ended` and the rest re-read through the same path.
- **The UI can't spin.** `TodayPlace` and `TodayScreen` poll on `deciding`, which is false here. `TodayPlace` says "A question is in progress on {topic}. What comes next is chosen when you finish it or go on." `DailyPlanView` says the same. Both show `resume ▸`, `set it aside` and Begin. `resume ▸` opens a problem on an item in practice and a Reader problem in its book; the classic Today can only do the former.
- **`plan_prefetch`.** New no-op reason `workbench_open`, checked straight after `not_a_boundary`.
- **What re-reads after a close, on the desktop.** `App.tsx`'s `useDailyPlan` stays mounted while a session is open and refetches when `TAG.queue` is invalidated. A submit invalidates it (`ATTEMPT_TAGS`). A set-aside invalidated nothing, so `rereadPlanAfterWorkbenchClosed` now does, on the `openItem` config key: off, closing refetches nothing, as before. That read crosses the boundary: it starts the job and answers `deciding`. Today polls until the block appears; on any other screen nothing re-reads, and feedback's prefetch or the next Next finds the job, as for any read today. So the job starts promptly, and the decision row is written at the next read after it finishes. On the phone, the next read or `plan_prefetch` after a close starts it.
- **Serves.** `serve` true never asks. Begin, Continue, the phone's Next and feedback's Next decide: `useNextPlanStep.resolve` serves when its read says `question_in_progress`, since feedback's Next is an explicit step and its read alone would land on Today. A serve leaves the problem open and resumable. Learner picks and "Something else would help" are as before.
- **The phone.** It has no workbench and reads under the desktop's. Its read holds at a boundary and says "A question is in progress on the desktop. Next here goes on without it." Its Next is a serve and decides, then its later reads are of the block in hand. A boundary reached there starts no prefetch, so that Next waits up to the 40 s of §3a.
- **IH2.** A drawn offer already waiting stays: a plan read makes no redraw, withdrawal or `ignored` sweep. A follow-up that becomes ready waits, and the first read after the close runs the contest (§5 item 1) or places it. What runs at the answer rather than on a read still decides then: `ObjectiveFollowupGate.decide_followup` after a miss, on an attempt, with or without a workbench open.
- **IH3.** The options read draws nothing and is unchanged; with no draw waiting it says `no_draw: none_waiting`. A choice is an explicit step and is served.
- **Long-lived sessions, with no time limit.** The session is restored when the app opens (`active` in the app snapshot), so one can span days, as on Nov 7-8. If its problem stays open, the next day's first read builds a plan with no block, and Today says a question is in progress, with resume, set aside and Begin. Nothing is drawn until the learner steps, and the first step decides on that day's evidence. That is the whole cost: a plan with no block until a step, and no prefetch, so that step waits on the models. No limit was needed, because reads are the only thing held. The hold ends with the session, since a new session's start ends the old one.
- **Limits.** (1) No record says a read was held: a log reader sees only a later `created_at`, and an empty `daily_plans` row that looks like a pending decision. (2) Membership is by `updated_at`, which a background import's bind also moves; a problem from an earlier session whose import lands in this one counts until it's closed. (3) With no session open, a Reader problem holds nothing: Today's read draws, as before. (4) A problem left open in the session keeps every boundary in that session unprefetched, so each Next there waits on the models. (5) Not verified in a running app: the look of the line on each surface.
- **Where the tests are.** `tests/test_ih6_workbench_open_item.py`, `TodayPlace.test.tsx`, `DailyPlanView.test.tsx`, `dailyPlan.test.tsx` and the phone's `Today.plan.test.tsx`.

**Tests:** a read with an open workbench writes no decision and starts no job; closing it lets the next read draw; a serve decides regardless; a workbench from an earlier session doesn't block; the Nov 8 shape draws after the attempt and has that attempt in its evidence; switch off and below the tag match the predecessor exactly.

## 7c. IH7: a lesson after a miss reads what the diagnosis points at

Under the unreleased `mvp-0.55` tag, with its own kill switch in `[teacher]` or `[router_objective]`, following how the neighbouring switches are placed. It changes what a lesson reads, not what the router decides. Off or below the tag, the brief is byte-identical to today's.

**Why.** On Nov 8 the offer was for rendering decision regions, whose linked section is a short code listing on p. 173 of the Welch Labs PDF. The confusion was softmax against `argmax`, and softmax is discussed in chapter 3, pp. 108-123. A lesson built only from the topic's links would reach that material only if the teacher chose to fetch it.

1. **When:** a lesson opened from an offer or a suggestion with a recent miss on its topics behind it. A lesson started from a Reader selection, or on a typed topic, is unchanged. A learner's selection stays first.
2. **What to search with:** the diagnosed confusion in the ledger for that miss: the diagnosis's hypotheses and the learner-text gaps' belief statements, in that order, including any the learner marked as `my_gap`.
3. **How to find sections:** the existing retrieval (`search_spans`, grouped to sections as `section_coverage` does) through the existing `Library`, so withheld and exam material, and exercises and solutions, stay out. Use existing per-source limits. The brief's "built deterministically from the ledger" holds unless the existing retrieval needs a model; if it does, report that before building.
4. **How they enter the brief:** beside the topic's linked sections, under the existing `teacher.context.sources_tokens` budget. A section that won't fit is listed in the outline, so the teacher can fetch it with `read_source`. The topic's linked sections are not displaced.
5. **Inspectable:** the brief records which sections were added, the confusion text that found each one, and why any were left out. If nothing matches, or the diagnosis is pending, failed or absent, the lesson opens as today and the record says so.
6. **Never:** a score or stuck flag choosing sections, or expected answers or rubrics entering the query.

**As built (IH7).** `teacher/diagnosed_sources.py` chooses the sections; `teacher/brief.py` renders them; `teacher/service.py::open_lesson` keeps the record. The switch is `[router_objective] lesson_sources_follow_diagnosis` (default true), on `mvp-0.55` with IH2, IH3, IH5 and IH6, in the parameter registry as an operational kill switch (`router_objective_switches.lesson_sources_follow_diagnosis_on`). It needs no objective and no lesson offers. Off, or below the tag, nothing here runs: the brief is byte-identical and the open turn carries no extra key (a test compares bytes).

- **No model call.** The existing retrieval can run without one: `search_sections(read_only=True)` is keyword (BM25) retrieval over the Library's scope, with no query embedding, no rewrite and no delivery log. IH7 always runs it that way, so the brief stays built deterministically from the ledger and the lesson's open never goes to the network, whatever `embedding.enabled` says or whether the query-embedding route is remote. The cost is recall: the query is the statement's words, with no semantic match, so a confusion phrased without the book's vocabulary finds nothing (the record then says `no section matched`), and an OR query over a long statement can also match loosely. Adding the embedding route later would be a separate, deliberate change.
- **Which lessons.** Only `origin = 'router'` with an `origin_ref`: a Start on an IH1 offer, an IH2 `plan_interrupt` offer or a TE1b suggestion (all `router_decisions` rows, whose response names the lesson's topics). A Reader selection, a typed topic and IH3's "Something else would help" are `learner_pick` with no `origin_ref` (the pick is logged in `router_learner_preferences`, but the lesson row does not link to it), so they are unchanged: the learner started them, and nothing on record says a miss is behind them.
- **The miss.** The latest attempt on the lesson's goal topics known at the decision's `created_at` (through the availability predicate, added with IH4 after `test_ro1_availability_reads` caught it): structure already on record, no time constant. It counts when a live diagnosis is in force for it (`Repository.current_live_diagnosis`, the rule every reader shares), so a correct attempt, which has none, adds nothing, and no score or stuck flag is read. A suggestion made long after a miss still reads that miss's confusion while it is the latest evidence on those topics; there is no age bound. An attempt on exam, held-out, reserved, cold-in-flight or diagnostic-probe material (`PastWork.attempt_reason`) ends it: the lesson opens as before.
- **The confusion.** In this order: the attempt's hypotheses (`causal_hypotheses`, latest version of each episode; those the learner marked `my_gap` first, then written order; a `retired` or `demoted` one only if marked `my_gap`), then its learner-text gaps (`learner_text_gaps`, kind prerequisite or misconception, not undone, the `named_json` belief statement). The diagnosis's free analysis (`analysis_md`), which may quote the expected answer, is not read. One search per statement, so each added section names the statement that found it.
- **Secrecy.** Expected answers and rubrics are never read. Each statement passes the shared protection (`learner_protection.PastWork.evidence_reason`: the attempt, the answers its episode rests on and its item), then its write-time record: one that names a protected answer, item, question or passage, or is incomplete, leaves the statement out and the record says so. A statement on an unprotected item can still restate what the diagnoser saw; that is the rule the router card and the figure author's learner state already apply, and the existing brief's `misconceptions` carry such text too. The Library removes withheld sections, exam and held-out material, and exercises and solutions before anything is returned, whatever the statement says.
- **Budget.** `_render_sources` shows the topic's linked sections first and decides whole or outline from them alone, as before; each added section then goes in whole only if it fits what is left of `teacher.context.sources_tokens`, else it is listed in the outline (`alternates`), where `read_source` reads it. Adding sections never demotes a linked one. A section the lesson already gets is not added twice. Up to `SECTIONS_PER_SOURCE` per source per statement come back, and the ones that fit are all shown whole, so a loose keyword match can bring in several sections.
- **Recap and protection.** Added passages get aliases in the same book as linked ones, so `revoke_unteachable`, `recap_admissible` (which reads that book from the open turn) and `read_source` apply unchanged; the miss's attempt is one of the brief's history entries, so its item is already named by the protection digest.
- **The record.** The teacher sees `sources.from_diagnosis` (`added` and `listed_in_outline`, each with the statement that found it) only when a section was added or listed. The full record, `sources_follow` in the open turn's payload (`lesson_turns` action `open`), says `added`, `listed` or `none` and why, the miss, the diagnosis, every statement with the sections it matched (or why it was withheld), and each section's outcome. Pending, failed, superseded or absent diagnoses, no miss, nothing matching: `none` with a reason, and the brief itself is unchanged.
- **Where the tests are.** `tests/test_ih7_lesson_sources_follow_diagnosis.py`.

**Tests:** synthetic fixture of the Nov 7 shape: a softmax confusion on a decision-regions topic brings in the section that discusses softmax, beside the linked one; a Reader selection stays first; withheld sections stay out; over budget, the section falls to the outline; no diagnosis means an unchanged brief; switch off and below the tag leave the brief byte-identical; the record names what was added and why.

## 10. Order and tests

Build order: IH0, IH1, IH5, IH2, IH3, IH6, IH7, IH4. IH0 and IH1 address the visible failures and change no decision rule. IH5 is small and independent. IH2 is the largest change to what the router decides. IH3's capture comes next, since data starts accruing as soon as it lands. IH6 and IH7 follow it (§7b and §7c hold their tests). IH4 needs IH3's data path and comes after IH6, because IH6 changes when decisions are drawn; its coefficient simply stays near 0 until data exists.

- **IH0:** `plan_prefetch` writes nothing in any decision table (row counts pinned across the call); a later serve finds the job and uses its answer without starting a second LLM call; it is a no-op in mid-block; it is allowlisted on the phone host; a slow fake LLM that answers within 40 s is served rather than falling back.
- **IH1:** a `lesson_offer` step opens the page; Start opens the lesson; `offer_shown` is written once per render path; no offer appears over an open item.
- **IH2:** replay this trace's shape, with a probe arriving during an open offer. The result must be a new decision holding both candidates with exact propensities, and never a `withdrawn` block with reason `forced_items`. A test also asserts that no code path maps a score or stuck flag directly to an action.
- **IH3:** an override serves the action, writes both rows in one transaction and is idempotent on retry; a preference with no draw is stored with a null `decision_id`; the phone hands a lesson pick to the desktop.
- **IH4:** with no preferences the decision matches the previous tag exactly; `w_pref` at 0 changes nothing (a lesson request's acceptance apart, §7); picks never enter `w`'s scoring or `w_pref`'s cohort.
- **IH5:** each row of §7a's table produces its weights, with the floor kept and exact propensities; a lesson offer in the support can be drawn with the LLM down; with the switch off or below the tag, the mix matches the predecessor exactly; a logged decision replays to its logged `μ` under its own tag.
